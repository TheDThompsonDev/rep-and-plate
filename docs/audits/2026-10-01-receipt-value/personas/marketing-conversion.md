# Persona 5 — Conversion and product marketing
Date: 2026-10-01. Perspective: a product marketer optimizing informed activation, proof and trust. This is a simulated expert review of current source, not a real user session, competitive market study, or measured conversion result. Parent browser observations and controlled tests are separate evidence; no real receipt accuracy is established by this review.

## Verdict
The app explains capture mechanics and the purchase/intake boundary, but its strongest receipt benefit arrives after onboarding. The likely conversion problem is an unclear exchange: a person gives a private shopping record, then performs product/quantity review, without first seeing what useful decision they will receive. Prioritize an honest sample journey and a small, explicit first reward.

Receipt photo → extracted purchases → check uncertain details → meal ideas from available food / reviewed trip spending → explicitly log eaten portions.

## 1. P1 — Ask for an account after showing a receipt outcome
**Observation.** Welcome leads with Spot's personality and Create account (`src/features/onboarding/Onboarding.tsx:158`, :166, :188). Four introduction slides describe food intake, workouts and recovery; none demonstrates receipt capture (`src/features/onboarding/model.ts:26`). The ready screen suggests one meal or movement (`src/features/onboarding/Onboarding.tsx:478`). Guest tracking is available, but AI requires an approved account (`src/features/onboarding/Onboarding.tsx:356`, :359).
**Hypothesis.** Someone evaluating receipt utility must invest in setup without knowing what account approval unlocks. Account creation alone cannot be treated as AI activation.
**Proposal and copy.** Add a pre-account, clearly labeled illustrative receipt journey, inspectable without uploading personal data: “Turn your grocery trip into a starting point for dinner.” “Photo → checked pantry → meal ideas using what you bought.” CTA: “See a sample receipt”; next CTA: “Set up my account.” Access note: “Receipt reading needs an approved account during private preview. You can explore this example without one.”
**Ethical test.** Compare current onboarding with an illustrative receipt example in consented sessions; ask participants to explain the benefit and account restriction before proceeding. Measure correct explanation, sample-to-setup continuation, approval availability and eventual receipt activation separately. No fictional testimonials, accuracy claims, or sample output presented as real recognition.

## 2. P1 — Make the immediate receipt reward legible at capture entry
**Observation.** Kitchen's action is “Add a receipt” (`src/KitchenPage.tsx:154`); nearby cards separately discuss swaps, budget and pantry (:158–183). A good benefit headline exists in the first-week section (`src/features/planning/FirstWeek.tsx:24`), but its initial next step can be preferences, preceding receipt (`src/features/planning/first-week.ts:27`, :34). Chat's About modal mentions receipt saving and meal ideas separately (`src/ChatLayer.tsx:905`).
**Hypothesis.** The user may read receipts as archival bookkeeping, rather than the input that connects shopping to practical food decisions.
**Proposal and copy.** Put one linked exchange alongside the first receipt entry, visible before the chooser: “Bought groceries? Start here.” “Read your receipt, check uncertain items, then use your groceries to plan meals and review this trip's spending.” CTA: “Add groceries from a receipt.” Keep manually entered pantry stock as a valid alternative; receipt benefits specifically include purchase information.
**Ethical test.** In first-use sessions, ask “What happens after you add this?” without suggesting the answer. Compare correct spontaneous benefit recall and choice to start; record declines. Measure reading clarity before click lift. No guaranteed savings or prevention-of-waste claim.

## 3. P1 — Promise an achievable first result and disclose review work
**Observation.** Preview promises identifying groceries and researching nutrition (`src/App.tsx:1856`), with generic “Send to Rep & Plate” (:1890). Product review can require matching the package and entering total servings (`src/features/products/ReceiptProductReview.tsx:55`, :78, :82). Pantry explicitly asks users to confirm product/nutrition, then quantities (`src/features/pantry/PantryDialog.tsx:348`). Representative receipt accuracy has not been evaluated (`docs/context/receipt-evaluation.md:3`).
**Hypothesis.** Nutrition research sounds like a finished answer while the next screen asks for substantive checking. That gap can turn a successful upload into perceived failure.
**Proposal and copy.** Receipt-specific CTA: “Read my receipt.” Supporting copy: “We'll save the items we can read and flag uncertain details. Check products and amounts before using nutrition or planning.” After extraction: “Start with the foods you want to use next. Leave unknown amounts blank.” Partial progress must remain accurately labeled; do not imply unreviewed items are ready for nutrition planning.
**Ethical test.** Observe time and effort to reach one useful checked item and one usable meal idea, including correction and abandonment. Ask expected versus experienced effort. Compare explicit effort copy with current copy; favor comprehension and fewer surprise drop-offs, not upload rate alone. Never hide warnings to improve completion.

## 4. P1 — Reconcile processing reassurance with account saving
**Observation.** Capture correctly discloses AI processing but ends “Your saved records stay in this browser” (`src/App.tsx:1893`–:1895). Linked-account architecture also saves records/photos to a private account (`README.md:118`; cloud copy disclosure in `src/features/cloud/CloudAccount.tsx:504`). Provider retention limits are already documented (`README.md:120`).
**Hypothesis.** The capture wording can imply device-only storage to an account-linked user, undermining informed trust.
**Proposal and copy.** Make saving text conditional on verified account state, alongside: “This photo and relevant context are sent to our AI services for processing. Provider retention terms apply.” Local case: “Saved on this device.” Linked case: “Saved on this device and to your linked account when account saving succeeds.” Show the actual save state and a “How photos are handled” link. Explain what cropping excludes; do not promise that a crop deletes earlier copies.
**Ethical test.** Use an explicit consented comprehension study of destinations, retention and deletion expectations, with redacted samples. Do not A/B test truthful versus misleading disclosures. Success means users accurately understand processing and account saving; declining to upload is an acceptable outcome.

## 5. P2 — Treat the upload result as the start of payoff
**Observation.** GroceryCard confirms item count, review status and “Saved to groceries · not today's intake” (`src/AICards.tsx:111`–:120). It opens receipt details (:104), rather than directly presenting a food/spending decision. First-week steps show the longer path through reviewing, planning and shopping (`src/features/planning/first-week.ts:41`, :48, :56).
**Hypothesis.** A storage confirmation ends momentum before the user experiences why a receipt was worth uploading.
**Proposal and copy.** Keep the existing purchase/intake distinction and add status-based next actions: “Groceries saved. Check [actual count] uncertain items.” CTA: “Check items for dinner ideas.” When usable reviewed pantry exists: “Use these groceries for meal ideas.” Separate optional CTA: “Review this trip's spending”; do not present extracted prices as reviewed. Each action should open the relevant review/planner, with clear unknowns and a return path.
**Ethical test.** Primary activation event: user reviews a receipt-derived item and uses it in a reviewed plan or confirms a meaningful spending view. Count meal ideas merely opened separately from plans approved. With optional consent, log event names/counts and coarse timing, never raw receipt text, images, account email or health data. Monitor corrections, misunderstood purchase-as-intake, and returns after review.

## Proposed measurement contract
Events describe real states: receipt entry seen; sample opened; eligibility checked; photo chosen; AI submission accepted; extraction returned/failed; item corrected/reviewed; price review saved; planner opened; plan reviewed/approved; portion logged. Funnel denominators include failures and ineligible users, separately segmented so a disabled private-preview account is not mistaken for a copy failure. Collect only the data needed for the experiment, disclose it, permit opt-out, set retention before collection, and avoid person-identifying payloads. Baselines, sample size and rollout decision rules must be established before interpreting uplift. No experiments were run or added by this persona.

## Evidence and limitations
Current web source and project documentation were read directly. Source references are current line locations, not proof that a user saw or understood a screen. Review does not establish live provider accuracy, speed, representative receipt coverage, exact market differentiation, monetary savings, privacy policy completeness or behavior on physical devices. No production source changed.
